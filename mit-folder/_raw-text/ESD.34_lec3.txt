z Functions should (initially) be expressed as solution
neutral statements, having no solution of specific 
function or form either explicit or implicit in the 
statement

Expressing Process - Limited Syntax
z The assertion is that all process can be reduced to one 
of a limited set
z Trying this forces you to consider if the step is truly a 
process, and of what limited type it might be
z One useful set, developed by Krumhauer, is:
– Channel (transport in place)
– Store (for a period of time)
– Change (in nature or type)
– Vary (in magnitude)
– Connect (multiple inputs to single outputs, or vice versa)
z Try it on your next opportunity set, and see if it works
Ref: Pahl and Beitz, after  Krumhauer

18Massachusetts Institute of Technology  © Ed Crawley 2007
Informational vs. Physical Processes and 
Objects - “Duality”
z Things (objects and processes) can be physical or informational
z Physical things deal with matter and energy, are “tangible”, and 
obey the “laws of nature”. Physical objects have mass 
(particle/wave), and occupy coordinates in space and time
z Informational things are not bound by the laws of nature. An 
informational object is a piece of information in the abstract (e.g. a 
database, an idea, a rule, a command). An informational process is 
some transformation of information (e.g. reading, storing, learning, 
creating, etc.)
z Informational things always have a physical manifestation 
somewhere (e.g. design → print, idea → neurons, rule → law book)
z Physical things implicitly contain the information necessary to 
describe them (but not derive them)
z Processes can have records, plans, etc which are also informational 
objects

45Massachusetts Institute of Technology  © Ed Crawley 2007
Expressing Concept
z There is no convention or standard for naming 
concepts, but they tend to be nouns or noun phrases
z Rationally, they should be named “operand + process + 
instrument” but few are
z They are often named by “operand + process + er” (lawn 
mower), but this often works only for the first such 
device (e.g. what is a people mover?)
z Other common patterns are the operand + instrument, or 
even just instrument
z New concepts can be expressed by a few words or a 
short phrase (e.g. cell phone)
z Established concepts can often be expressed by a word 
or two or an icon (e.g. Refrigerator,                 )
